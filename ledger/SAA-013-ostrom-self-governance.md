## Claim
Communities can govern shared resources sustainably — avoiding *both* a tyrant/privatizer and a
tragedy-of-the-commons collapse — when ~8 design principles hold: clear boundaries; rules matched
to local conditions; collective-choice arrangements; monitoring; **graduated sanctions**; cheap
conflict resolution; recognized rights to organize; and nested governance for larger systems.

## Evidence
Synthesized from ~800 cases (irrigation, forests, fisheries, pastures); a meta-analysis (Cox et
al. 2010, 91 studies) found strong-to-moderate support, and failures correlate with principle
violations. **Scope limit (honest):** validated for *local/regional* commons with face-to-face
accountability; the principles do **not** straightforwardly scale to *global* open-access commons
(climate, high seas) — so this is **ROBUST (local) / scope-limited (global)**.

## Why it matters
A measured, non-utopian model of decentralized governance that resists capture without collapsing
into disorder — the institutional companion to [[SAA-012-reverse-dominance-hierarchy]]. It also
supplies the design grammar (monitoring, graduated sanctions, nested governance) for the kind of
open, accountable information commons this project tries to build — at the scale where it actually works.
