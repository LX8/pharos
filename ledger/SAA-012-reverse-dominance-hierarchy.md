## Claim
Boehm argues that egalitarian hunter-gatherer bands *actively and collectively* suppress
would-be dominators and bullies — through ridicule, criticism, ostracism, disobedience, and
ultimately sanction or killing — producing a "reverse dominance hierarchy" in which the rank
and file dominate would-be alphas. On this reading, **resisting a would-be tyrant is an evolved
human political behavior** — the deepest root of "containing authoritarianism."

## Evidence
Ethnographically documented across many foraging societies and seriously engaged (13 peer
commentaries on the 1993 paper). **But the re-check downgraded this from ROBUST to CONTESTED:**
its *universality* is genuinely disputed — Erdal & Whiten propose "counterdominance" as a
different mechanism, and Graeber & Wengrow marshal evidence that foragers were *not* uniformly
egalitarian (seasonal shifts between flat and hierarchical organization; contemporary foragers
may be marginal "refugee" cases, not representative of the ancestral range).

## Why it matters
A compelling and on-thesis deep root — humans have an evolved capacity to coordinate against
domination — but a *contested* one. Keep it as motivation and hypothesis, not settled fact; and
note its honest limit even where supported: band-level levelling does not automatically scale to
the modern state (the project's actual problem).
