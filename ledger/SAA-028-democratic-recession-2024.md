## Claim
The descriptive trend is ROBUST: as of the 2024-2026 V-Dem and Freedom House reports, two independent instruments converge on a multi-year autocratization wave — V-Dem's 2025 report finds autocracies (91) outnumber democracies (88) for the first time in ~20 years, ~72% of world population lives in autocracies (up from ~49% in 2004), 45 countries are autocratizing, and population-weighted liberal democracy is back to mid-1980s levels; Freedom House records a 19th consecutive annual decline. The SECOND layer — whether this constitutes a genuine *global* recession rather than partly a measurement/perception effect — is CONTESTED: Little & Meng (2024) show objective electoral indicators are roughly flat and posit time-varying expert "bad-vibes" coder bias, but Knutsen et al. (2024) find little to no evidence for that specific bias and conclude the balance of evidence indicates real backsliding. Net grade ROBUST for the measured trend; the live scholarly debate is narrowed to magnitude, scope of dimensions, and weighting of non-electoral institutional erosion rather than to whether decline exists at all.

## Evidence
FOR: V-Dem (Nord, Angiolillo, Good God & Lindberg 2025, Democratization, using V-Dem v15) and Freedom House (Freedom in the World 2025) are methodologically independent (expert-survey IRT model vs. analyst-checklist) yet agree on direction, multi-year duration, and rough magnitude — convergent validity. Knutsen et al. (2024, 10 V-Dem-affiliated authors) ran direct tests for the coder-bias mechanism Little & Meng proposed and found "little to no empirical evidence" for it; they conclude "the balance of evidence clearly indicates that there is recent global democratic backsliding." In their own reply, Little & Meng concede their objective index captures "only part of the picture" (electoral competition, not civil liberties / executive constraints), that expansive definitions show decline, and that "any amount of backsliding is a reasonable cause for concern." AGAINST / caveats: Little & Meng (2024) show objective metrics (incumbent vote share, turnover, term-limit evasion) are roughly stable over the past decade — "democratic stability or stagnation rather than decline." Expert-coded indices require subjective judgment and the IRT model assumes time-invariant coder standards, a genuine validity concern; population-weighted headlines are dominated by a few large states (India, US), so "72% live in autocracies" is sensitive to a handful of borderline classifications and to where the democracy/autocracy threshold is drawn (conceptual-stretching risk). Reports are also produced by advocacy-adjacent institutions, inviting motivated-reasoning scrutiny.

## Why it matters
This is the empirical backbone of the "containing authoritarianism" agenda: if the global recession is largely a measurement or perception artifact, early-warning and intervention frameworks risk false alarms; if it is real and accelerating, the case for monitoring is strong. The layered grade lets the ledger assert the trend confidently while honestly flagging that the magnitude and the population-weighted headline depend on contestable coding and threshold choices. This corroborates the project's own indicators — the WGI six-dimension finding ([`../docs/indicators/wgi-governance.md`](../docs/indicators/wgi-governance.md)) and the voice-divergence triangulation — which deliberately publish *divergence, not a single score*; so the 72% population-weighted figure is V-Dem's headline framing, not a blended number this project would publish.
