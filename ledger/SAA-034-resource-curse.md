## Claim
The claim splits by layer. The cross-national negative ASSOCIATION between oil/mineral rents and democracy is ROBUST and heavily replicated (Ross 2001; confirmed by Ahmadov's 2014 meta-analysis). But the CAUSAL "unconditional resource curse" is CONTESTED: Haber & Menaldo (2011, APSR) show the association largely vanishes once long-run country fixed effects / pre-resource trends are absorbed, while Andersen & Ross (2014) rebut them, showing a pronounced curse emerges post-1980 once developing states captured rents from foreign firms. Net: a real, time-varying, conditional effect — not a fabricated correlation, but not a clean universal causal law either.

## Evidence
FOR the curse: Ross (2001, World Politics) finds oil/mineral reliance lowers democracy net of controls, beyond the Middle East and small states, via rentier (low-tax/patronage), repression, and modernization channels. Ahmadov's (2014) meta-regression over the published literature finds a "nontrivial negative association" globally, conditional on region/institutions. Andersen & Ross (2014, CPS) show a "pronounced resource curse" since ~1980. AGAINST (the revisionist layer): Haber & Menaldo (2011, APSR) argue resource reliance is endogenous, so cross-country panels suffer omitted-variable bias from unobserved heterogeneity; using long historical within-country series they find no authoritarian effect and sometimes a "resource blessing." The genuine dispute is identification: H&M's fixed-effects/historical design vs. A&R's claim that those very models mask a real post-1970s, time-heterogeneous effect. Both are top-journal, peer-reviewed, and the exchange remains live.

## Why it matters
Resource rents are a core mechanism by which regimes finance repression and buy acquiescence without taxation, weakening the "no taxation without representation" accountability link. Whether the curse is universal or conditional shapes whether anti-authoritarian strategy should target rent capture itself, or the institutional/temporal conditions (clientelist vs. contract-intensive economies, rent ownership) under which rents corrode democracy.
