## Claim
The claim is correct as stated when read as a CONDITIONAL effect, and that reading is the defensible one. Grade splits by layer: (a) the existence of a backfire mechanism — repression seen as illegitimate, visible, and hitting integrated/identified groups can activate micromobilization and INCREASE protest — is ROBUST and broadly accepted. (b) The notion that backfire is a universal or even modal effect of repression is REFUTED: repression also routinely deters and demobilizes, and which outcome dominates is contingent. (c) The net/average sign of the repression-dissent relationship is OPEN/CONTESTED — this is the field's long-standing "punishment puzzle," with findings ranging from negative to positive to curvilinear (inverted-U).

## Evidence
FOR (mechanism): Opp & Roehl (1990) show repression's direct deterrent effect is neutralized or reversed when targets perceive it as illegitimate and belong to protest-supportive networks — coining the micromobilization conditions the claim names. Hess & Martin (2006) formalize "backfire": repression backfires when the act is visible/known AND perceived as unjust (e.g., Dharasana 1930, Dili 1991). These map directly onto the claim's listed conditions (illegitimacy, visibility, collective identity). AGAINST (universality): Davenport's (2007) review names the "punishment puzzle" — repression empirically sometimes raises and sometimes lowers dissent, with no consensus average effect; relative-deprivation models predict an inverted-U (moderate represses mobilizes, severe deters). Honari (2018) documents the evidence as genuinely contradictory (negative, positive, curvilinear, and substitution-to-other-tactics findings all appear), and stresses that PERCEIVED repression can decouple from objective macro-level repression — undercutting any clean universal law. So the strongest evidence both supports the mechanism AND refutes the universal-effect reading.

## Why it matters
For containing authoritarianism, this is load-bearing and double-edged. It warrants the strategic logic that visible, disproportionate crackdowns on peaceful, legitimate dissent can be self-defeating for a regime (backfire) — but it does NOT license the comforting narrative that repression always strengthens a movement. Severe, indiscriminate, low-visibility, or fear-saturating repression frequently works to demobilize. Overselling backfire as universal would mislead organizers into underestimating real risk and would misread the empirical record.
