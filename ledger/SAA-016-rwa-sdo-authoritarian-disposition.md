## Claim
As individual-difference predictors, Altemeyer's Right-Wing Authoritarianism (RWA) and Pratto & Sidanius's Social Dominance Orientation (SDO) are robustly and repeatedly associated with prejudice, intergroup hostility, and anti-democratic/pro-authoritarian attitudes — this predictive core is ROBUST. The historical sub-claim is also accurate: Adorno's original F-scale was methodologically flawed (all items keyed in one direction, so high scores partly reflected acquiescence response bias), and Altemeyer's balanced RWA scale was the correction. One layer is genuinely CONTESTED — the interpretation of what the constructs ARE (stable personality traits vs. ideological attitudes) and a tautology concern that some RWA items resemble the prejudice outcomes they predict — but this debate is about interpretation/circularity, not about whether the measured association exists.

## Evidence
FOR: Sibley & Duckitt (2008), a meta-analysis of 71 studies (N=22,068), establishes RWA and SDO as the two dominant ideological predictors of generalized prejudice, with personality effects on prejudice largely mediated through them (Openness via RWA, Agreeableness via SDO). Pratto et al. (1994) originally showed SDO predicts racism, sexism, nationalism, and support for hierarchy-enhancing policy across multiple samples; Duckitt's dual-process model replicates the additive RWA+SDO structure widely, and the two predict support for authoritarian leaders, political intolerance, and illiberal policy. The acquiescence critique of the F-scale (Bass 1955; Couch & Keniston 1960) is textbook-confirmed, and the balanced RWA scale (Altemeyer 1981) is its acknowledged methodological fix with alpha typically .85-.94. AGAINST: A real, ongoing critique holds that RWA's unidimensional scale conflates three theorized factors and that some items are near-tautological with the prejudice criteria they predict; relatedly, critics argue the constructs are ideological positions intrinsic to conservatism rather than content-free personality traits — which would inflate the predictive headline if the predictor and outcome overlap conceptually.

## Why it matters
Containing authoritarianism requires knowing who is disposed toward anti-democratic, pro-hierarchy, and intolerant positions and why. RWA and SDO are the field's two most validated dispositional instruments for that purpose, and the dual-process finding that they are differently motivated (RWA by threat/security, SDO by competitive dominance) implies different de-escalation levers. Conflating the robust predictive core with the contested personality-vs-ideology interpretation would either overstate the science (claiming a fixed authoritarian personality type) or understate it (dismissing well-replicated predictors).
