## Claim
War is not a human inevitability: many **non-warring societies are ethnographically documented**,
and violence is not "genetically programmed." Therefore an enduring reduction in organized
violence is *possible*, not utopian.

## Evidence
- **ROBUST core:** peaceful/non-warring societies demonstrably exist (Fry's catalogues).
- **CONTESTED:** *how frequent and how ancient* war was before the Neolithic is a genuine,
  unresolved debate — Fry (war is recent and rare ancestrally) vs Gat/Bowles (older, more
  frequent); the skeletal record is fragmentary and read both ways.
- **The Seville Statement** ("war is not biologically obligatory") has a *surviving core* but its
  wording **overstated** the absence of any genetic influence. The modern synthesis is
  gene × environment: violence is a *canalized potential*, strongly modulated by culture — neither
  innately-warlike nor blank-slate. (So: an overstatement, not a refutation.)

## Why it matters
Grounds the project's hope in measured fact (peace is achievable) while refusing the over-claim
(that humans are "naturally peaceful"). The honest, defensible position — *violence is neither
inevitable nor impossible; it is conditioned* — is itself the case for working on the conditions.
