## Claim
When judging an unfamiliar website or source, the expert move is **lateral reading** — quickly *leaving*
the page to see what independent, reliable sources say *about* it — rather than **vertical reading**
(staying on the page and scrutinizing its design, "About" page, or references). Professional
fact-checkers read laterally and reach accurate credibility judgments fast; Stanford undergraduates and
even PhD historians mostly read vertically and do markedly worse. The strategy is **teachable**:
curricula that explicitly teach lateral reading measurably improve students' credibility judgments
from before to after instruction. The ROBUST core is the *expert-strategy* finding plus the *measured instructional gain*;
the **durability and real-world transfer** of the taught skill is a genuine open question, held weaker by
analogy to the prebunking-transfer caveat ([[SAA-003]]) — so the claim is "improves evaluation in
studies," not "durably immunizes in the wild."

## Evidence
FOR (the expert strategy): Wineburg & McGrew (2019, *Teachers College Record*) put professional
fact-checkers, Stanford students, and historians through the same source-evaluation tasks; the
fact-checkers' lateral reading made them far faster and more accurate, while subject-matter expertise
(the historians) did *not* confer web-evaluation skill — the skill is procedural, not topical. FOR (it is
teachable): McGrew & Breakstone (2023, *AERA Open*) find that lessons embedding lateral reading across a
curriculum measurably improve students' online-reasoning judgments **from pretest to posttest** (N ≈ 574),
consistent with the broader lateral-reading intervention literature. CAVEAT: as with inoculation, effect
*durability* and *transfer* to everyday
behavior outside the assessment are less established and vary across studies — this is the same honest
open edge graded in [[SAA-003]], not a contradiction of the core.

## Why it matters
This is the epistemic-defense thesis made **operational**: a concrete, learnable habit that helps people
resist manipulation, and one that scales through ordinary curricula rather than requiring platform
cooperation. It complements inoculation/"prebunking" ([[SAA-002]]) — inoculation raises resistance to
manipulation techniques in the abstract; lateral reading supplies the verification *routine* a person
actually runs on a live, unfamiliar source. Open, checkable knowledge is the antidote to a politics that
subordinates truth to power, and lateral reading is how an individual practises it.
