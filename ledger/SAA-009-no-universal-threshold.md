## Claim
The popular "magic number" reading is false. Measured thresholds range from <1%
to ~40–65% depending on context, network, incentives, and what is changing
(Xie ~10% in models; Centola ~25% in lab; Chenoweth ~3.5% descriptive for
regime change; ~50–65% for some real behaviors). *Who* and *where* in the
network matters as much as *how many*. And reaching critical mass does **not**
guarantee a cascade: cascades are *rare* (most seeds fizzle — Watts) and
beatable — nonviolent-campaign success fell from ~65% (1990s) to under 34%
(2010s) as regimes learned disinformation, surveillance, and cross-border
repression.

## Evidence
Convergent across the collective-action and network literatures; the
declining-success finding is from Chenoweth's own updated data.

## Why it matters
Keeps the [`SAA-008`](SAA-008-critical-mass-cascades-are-real.md) mechanism from
becoming over-claim. Strategy must target network position, commitment, and
reinforcement — and must defend against counter-forces — not chase a number.
