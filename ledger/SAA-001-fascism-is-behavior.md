## Claim
Fascism is best defined, following Paxton, as a form of political *behavior* —
obsessive preoccupation with community decline/victimhood plus compensatory
cults of unity, purity, and a leader — pursued by a mass party of nationalist
militants in collaboration with traditional elites, abandoning democratic
liberties and pursuing redemptive violence. It runs on **mobilizing passions**,
and treats truth as *instrumental*: it makes many checkable factual claims
(inflated statistics, fabricated results, denied atrocities) but is indifferent
to whether they hold — the currency is loyalty-signalling and demonstrated
dominance, not belief.

## Evidence
Verified 3–0 against the primary text (definition p.~218; "mobilizing passions"
and "affair of the gut" p.41; five-stage process p.23).

## Why it matters
Fascism is affective more than propositional — but the lever is **not** simply
refuting its claims. The signalling literature (Huang 2015) shows a lie's
*falsity can be the point* (public assent to an obvious falsehood demonstrates
dominance), so refutation alone does not disarm it. What open, auditable
knowledge does is narrower and real: it **denies fascism the cover of
manufactured ignorance** and gives institutions and the still-persuadable
auditable ground to stand on — it does not by itself move the captured.
Definitions are genuinely contested (Griffin, Eco, Mann), so this is the
rigorous **anchor**, not a claim of universal consensus; Paxton himself warns
against naive checklist scoring.
