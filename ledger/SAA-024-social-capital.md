## Claim
The association between social capital and democratic governance, institutional performance, and civic participation is robust and replicates across Putnam's Italian regions and cross-national samples. The overall claim grades contested because two layers are disputed. First, the causal direction; the bottom-up story that civic society builds good institutions is challenged by an institutionalist school finding the reverse, that institutions generate trust. Second, the secular decline thesis associated with Bowling Alone, whose magnitude is over-stated and uneven across indicators.

## Evidence
For the association, Putnam 1993 found civic Italian regions outperformed less-civic ones under identical institutions, and Knack and Keefer 1997 found trust and civic norms predict performance across 29 economies. Against causation, Knack and Keefer did not rule out reverse causality, and Rothstein and Martinangeli show institutional quality causes trust. Against decline, Costa and Kahn 2001 call it over-stated and Paxton found trust fell while membership held steady.

## Why it matters
Social capital underpins arguments that civil society inoculates democracies against authoritarian capture. Misreading the robust association as a settled bottom-up causal mechanism licenses a fix-civil-society-first prescription, whereas the institutionalist evidence implies building impartial institutions is higher-leverage. Invoking a sweeping decline narrative as fact overstates the data.
