## Claim
Human cooperation and the norms that sustain it are **culturally evolved and transmitted** —
institutions, kinship structures, and norm-enforcement shape psychology (including the unusually
individualistic, impersonally-prosocial, trust-of-strangers profile of "WEIRD" populations).

## Evidence
- **ROBUST framework:** convergent lab, ethnographic, archaeological, and historical evidence that
  cultural transmission of norms drives cross-population variation in cooperation; the WEIRD
  pattern itself is well documented.
- **CONTESTED mechanism:** Henrich's specific causal story (the Church's dissolution of intensive
  kinship → markets → WEIRD psychology) is debated and incomplete — critics note it underweights
  colonialism/empire and the persistence of ritualized hierarchy under capitalism.

## Why it matters
If cooperation and anti-domination norms are *built*, not fixed, they can be *cultivated* — which
is the premise of the whole project. It also sets the honest limit: norms are slow, path-dependent,
and culture-specific, so interventions must be humble about transplanting one society's institutions
onto another. Pairs with [[SAA-012-reverse-dominance-hierarchy]] and [[SAA-013-ostrom-self-governance]].
