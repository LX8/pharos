## Claim
The predictive claim is robust: scores on Kteily et al.'s "Ascent of Man" measure predict support for hostile, aggressive, and exclusionary policies (torture, collective punishment, retaliatory violence, denial of aid, educational tracking discrimination) over and above subtle prejudice and general dislike, replicated across multiple targets, countries, and at least one real behavioral outcome. The claim splits by layer: the incremental-predictive-validity layer is ROBUST, but the deeper interpretive layer — that the measure indexes a distinct "denial of humanity" construct rather than a strong/extreme form of antipathy — is CONTESTED in a live divergent-validity debate.

## Evidence
FOR: Kteily, Bruneau, Waytz & Cotterill (2015, JPSP) introduced and validated the Ascent measure across 7 studies in 3 countries, showing it uniquely predicts aggressive/exclusionary attitudes "above prejudice." Kteily & Bruneau (2017, PSPB "Backlash") extended this to real-world targets (Mexican immigrants, Muslims) and aggressive-policy endorsement. Bruneau, Szekeres, Kteily, Tropp & Kende (2020, GPIR) is the strongest test: Hungarian preservice teachers' blatant dehumanization of Roma predicted actual discriminatory school-tracking decisions, whereas subtle dehumanization and general dislike did NOT. AGAINST: divergent-validity critics (Enock et al. 2021; Over 2021) argue dehumanization measures are confounded with antipathy/antisocial-trait attribution and may not be a distinct construct. Izydorczak, Grzyb & Dolinski (2022, Collabra), a preregistered Polish sample (N=1,751), raised methodological/ethical concerns and documented large score inflation (most respondents show no dehumanization), questioning the measure's distributional and interpretive properties — though their specific predicted artifacts (slider position, display order, attitude-shifting from participation) were disconfirmed.

## Why it matters
If explicit ratings of an out-group as "less than human" forecast support for hostile and exclusionary policies independent of ordinary prejudice, blatant dehumanization is a measurable early-warning signal for the kind of mass-exclusion and violence-licensing that accompanies authoritarian and genocidal politics — actionable for monitoring elite and public rhetoric.
