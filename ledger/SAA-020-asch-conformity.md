## Claim
The core effect is ROBUST: a unanimous incorrect majority reliably induces a substantial minority of people to endorse a transparently wrong perceptual judgment. Asch's original studies found conformity on roughly a third of critical trials (~37%) with ~75% of participants conforming at least once; a 133-study meta-analysis (mean ~31%) and a 2023 pre-registered-style PLOS ONE replication (33%, called a "perfect replication" of Asch's 36.8%) confirm it holds across decades. The magnitude moderators are weaker: the culture gradient (collectivist > individualist) and the U.S. era-decline are real descriptive patterns but rest on correlational, ecological evidence (Hofstede scores, confounds with study features), so those interpretive layers are CONTESTED, not robust.

## Evidence
FOR the core effect: Asch (1956) reported ~37% conformity on critical trials and ~75% conforming at least once, vs. <1% error in control. Bond & Smith (1996) meta-analyzed 133 line-judgment studies across 17 countries (overall ~31.2% conformity, range 14% Belgian students to 58% Fijian Indian teachers). Franzen & Mader (2023, PLOS ONE, N=202) found 33% error in the standard condition, statistically indistinguishable from Asch's 36.8% (t(16)=0.59, p=0.57), and extended it to political opinions (38%). FOR the moderators: Bond & Smith found conformity correlated with Hofstede collectivism and lower in individualist samples (~25% N.America/NW Europe), and declining in U.S. studies since the 1950s. AGAINST/limits: moderator claims are ecological correlations vulnerable to confounds (era, task version, in-group vs out-group majority, publication era); the culture and decline findings have never been tested with a controlled cross-national experiment, and effect size depends heavily on majority unanimity (a single dissenter collapses conformity).

## Why it matters
Asch is the foundational demonstration that social pressure can override the plain evidence of one's senses, a mechanism directly relevant to how authoritarian systems manufacture apparent consensus and suppress dissent. The single-dissenter finding — that one ally drastically reduces conformity — is the load-bearing, replicated result for any theory of how visible non-compliance protects against coerced conformity. Overstating the culture/era moderators would, however, license simplistic "some cultures are inherently more obedient" narratives the data do not support.
