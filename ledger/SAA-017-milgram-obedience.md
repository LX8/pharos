## Claim
The claim splits cleanly by layer. EFFECT layer (ROBUST): under situational pressure from an authority, a large fraction of ordinary people will administer what they believe are harmful/escalating shocks — Burger (2009) found 70% continued to/past 150V in a partial US replication, and Dolinski et al. (2017) found ~90% delivered the maximum shock in Poland, broadly consistent across cultures and 50+ years. INTERPRETATION layer (CONTESTED): the popular "blind obedience to authority" reading is genuinely disputed. The engaged-followership / social-identification account (Haslam & Reicher) and Perry's archival work argue participants are moved by identification with the scientific enterprise and persuasive appeals, not by deference to orders — notably the most order-like prod ('You have no other choice, you must go on') was the LEAST effective. Because a robust behavioral effect coexists with a live, unresolved mechanism debate, the overall entry is CONTESTED, with the effect itself flagged as the robust core.

## Evidence
FOR the effect: Burger (2009, American Psychologist) replicated to the 150V threshold and found obedience only modestly below Milgram (70% vs ~82.5% at the equivalent point), concluding the result is not era-bound. Dolinski et al. (2017, SPPS) found ~90% delivered the maximum shock in Poland. The paradigm has replicated directly and conceptually across decades and cultures. AGAINST the effect: none of these overturn that ordinary people comply at high rates — this layer survives adversarial reading. FOR the standard interpretation being wrong: Haslam, Reicher & Birney (2014, J. Social Issues) showed the prod most resembling a direct order produced the LEAST continuation, undercutting an obedience-to-orders mechanism; Reicher, Haslam & Smith (2012) reframe the data as identification-based 'engaged followership'; Perry's archival audit found Milgram's experimenter improvised off-script and that the headline rate was condition-dependent (most subjects across the full set of 24 conditions did not fully obey). AGAINST (defending the orthodox reading): Hollander & Turowetz's 'multiple compliant processes' reply (2018, BJSP) argues followership is one of several mechanisms, not a wholesale replacement — i.e. the debate is genuinely two-sided and unresolved, which is why this is CONTESTED rather than REFUTED.

## Why it matters
Containing authoritarianism rests partly on a model of why people carry out harmful orders. If destructive compliance flows from active identification with a legitimating project (science, nation, party) rather than passive deference to authority, then countermeasures shift: from 'reduce orders / insert a chain-of-command circuit-breaker' toward 'deny perpetrators a legitimating shared identity and a virtuous-sounding cause.' Over-citing 'blind obedience' as settled science would mis-target interventions and overstate how mechanical the process is.
