## Claim
The graded claim is **descriptive and mechanism-level**: since the end of the Cold War, the dominant
*mode* of democratic erosion has shifted from abrupt seizures — military coups, "executive coups," and
blatant election-day fraud, all now declining — to **gradual, incumbent-led "executive aggrandizement,"**
in which leaders elected through legitimate means then incrementally weaken the checks on their power
(courts, legislatures, the civil service, independent media, election administration) through nominally
legal, piecemeal steps, alongside strategic electoral manipulation. This is a robust typological finding
about *how* backsliding happens, and it is **non-partisan**: the pathway is defined by the erosion of
constraints, independent of the incumbent's ideology. (How *much* democracy has declined globally — the
magnitude — is a separate and genuinely **contested** question, graded in [[SAA-028]]; this entry grades
the mechanism, not the magnitude.)

## Evidence
FOR: Bermeo (2016, *Journal of Democracy*), working from coded data on seizures of power, documents that
classic coups and open-ended power grabs have become rarer while *promissory coups*, *executive
aggrandizement*, and *strategic electoral manipulation* have become the characteristic forms — erosion
that is gradual and often legal rather than a single dramatic rupture. Lührmann & Lindberg (2019,
*Democratization*), using V-Dem data, independently characterize the contemporary "third wave of
autocratization" as **gradual and led by incumbents** who are first elected and then hollow institutions
from within — explicitly unlike the abrupt coups of earlier waves. The two literatures, built on
different data, converge on the same mechanism. COMPLICATION: the typology describes *how* erosion
proceeds, not *whether* a given country is eroding (a measurement question with real expert-coding
debate — see [[SAA-028]]); and not every case fits cleanly (some hybrid or abrupt cases remain).

## Why it matters
It tells defenders **where to look**. The threat to a modern democracy rarely arrives as tanks in the
street; it arrives as the slow, legalistic capture of the courts, the election authority, the public
broadcaster, and the civil service. Asking "was there a coup?" (usually no) misses it; asking "is
aggrandizement underway?" catches it early. This is exactly the institutional signal an open,
uncertainty-honest early-warning layer should track ([[SAA-007]]) — and the reason the project measures
*the conduct of power* (courts, voice, accountability), not people.
