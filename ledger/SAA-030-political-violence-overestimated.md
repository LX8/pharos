## Claim
The measurement-correction core is ROBUST: standard self-report survey estimates of support for partisan political violence (often cited at 8-40%) are substantially inflated by inattentive/bogus responding, expressive responding, and ambiguous "violence" wording; with attentive respondents and act-specific, severity-disambiguated items, support for serious violence (assault, murder) falls to low single digits and most partisans do not endorse it. The magnitude and the "most ordinary partisans reject violence" interpretation are CONTESTED: critics (Kalmoe & Mason) argue Westwood et al. overstate the novelty and partly attack a straw man, and independent nationally representative surveys (UC Davis/Wintemute) still find a non-negligible minority endorsing specific violent acts. Grade splits by layer: effect of measurement error = ROBUST; exact corrected level and downstream framing = CONTESTED.

## Evidence
FOR: Westwood et al. 2022 (PNAS, 4 experiments, n=4,904) show inattentive respondents and hypothetical/ambiguous wording drive estimates up; with engaged respondents and act-specific items, support for assault/murder is minimal while apparent "support" partly reflects property crime or non-violent acts. Independent corroboration: Separately, cognitive-interview work has found ~33% raw Democratic agreement that "force is justified to remove Trump" collapsed to ~8% endorsing violence under current conditions; ~4-7% of online respondents are "bogus." UC Davis/Wintemute longitudinal surveys flag the same low-prevalence data-quality problem. AGAINST: Kalmoe & Mason's PNAS letter ("A holistic view of conditional American support for political violence") argues Westwood et al. largely replicate their own engaged-respondent findings, counter a straw man, and that conditional, act-specific support remains non-trivial. UC Davis surveys still record a meaningful minority (e.g. ~quarter endorsing violence for at least one objective in some items) — so "far lower" is real but the residual is not zero.

## Why it matters
Headline "X% of Americans support political violence" statistics are routinely used to argue democratic erosion or to justify securitized responses; if those numbers are measurement artifacts inflated 3-10x, both threat assessments and counter-messaging strategies built on them are miscalibrated. Correcting the metric matters for not manufacturing a false sense of mass appetite for violence while still tracking the genuine, smaller hardcore. Cross-reference [`SAA-027`](SAA-027-depolarization-megastudy.md), whose megastudy reports a small treatment-induced *increase* in violence-support among some conservatives — the same low-prevalence-measurement caution applies there.
