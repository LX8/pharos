## Claim
Grade splits by layer. The descriptive ideology effect — liberals lean on individualizing foundations (care/fairness) while conservatives spread more evenly across all foundations — replicates and is ROBUST. But the specific claims this entry asks about fail: the original five-factor structure of the MFQ does NOT cleanly replicate in independent samples (reanalyses recover 2-3 factors or fail to converge), and the strong "innate, cross-culturally stable" universality claim is REFUTED by a direct 27-country invariance test. The theory's own authors conceded the original instrument's problems by building the MFQ-2 (now six foundations), whose nomological network still varies across cultures. Overall CONTESTED: a real construct and a robust ideology correlate, wrapped in an oversold structural-and-universality headline.

## Evidence
FOR: Graham, Haidt & Nosek (2009, JPSP 96:1029) found across 4 methods (relevance ratings, judgments, taboo trade-offs, sermon text) that liberals weight care/fairness more and conservatives weight all five more equally — independently replicated (e.g. Frimer 2020 text-analysis replications). Graham et al. (2011, JPSP 101:366) report CFA justification for a 5-factor MFQ in their derivation samples. AGAINST: Iurino & Saucier (2020, Assessment 27:365) tested measurement invariance across 27 countries and concluded the 5-factor MFQ cannot be specified to converge nonproblematically across diverse populations — invariance fails. Harper & Rhodes (2021, Br J Soc Psychol 60:1303) reanalysing UK voters recovered only three clusters (traditionalism, compassion, liberty), not the theorised structure. Atari et al. (2023, JPSP, MFQ-2) implicitly concede the original instrument's poor fit — they rebuilt it, split Fairness into Equality+Proportionality (six foundations), and show the moral nomological network VARIES by culture, contradicting strong universality even as it supports moral pluralism.

## Why it matters
MFT is heavily invoked to explain political polarization and the moral appeal of authoritarian/populist movements (binding foundations — loyalty, authority, purity — as the substrate that strongmen mobilize). If the instrument's factor structure and cross-cultural universality do not hold, then cross-national or longitudinal claims built on MFQ scores (e.g. "rising authoritarianism tracks binding-foundation endorsement") rest on a measure that is not comparable across the very groups being contrasted. The robust descriptive ideology effect can be used; the innate-universal framing cannot be load-bearing.
