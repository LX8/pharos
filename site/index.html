01What the evidence actually shows

We measured democratic governance across six World Bank dimensions for ~204 countries, 2014–2024 — and we kept the uncertainty. The honest headline is narrower, and more useful, than the doom-or-denial you usually read:

88%

of ~1,200 country-by-dimension changes are within measurement noise — most of the world did not measurably move in a decade.

  • Within noise
  • Real declines (lead improvements 88:54)
  • Real improvements

Of the changes that are real, the erosion is concentrated in one place: Voice & Accountability — free expression, free media, participation, the accountability of power.

We flag a change only when it beats its 90% confidence interval — and the voice decline survives the stricter 95% bar too. And we don’t ask you to trust one number: we put the same question to three independent instruments (the World Bank, V-Dem, and Freedom House) and publish where they agree and where they conflict. Every instrument that registers a significant change concurs on a decline in voice in 45 countries — none dissenting, and in 26 of them all three are independently significant; the handful where they genuinely disagree are flagged as conflicts, never averaged into a false consensus. A gamed single source surfaces as a conflict, not a clean number.

How the measurement works — in one paragraph

For each country and governance dimension, we take the change in the published 0–100 score from 2014 to 2024. The World Bank publishes a 90% confidence interval with every score; from it we derive the standard error and test the change with a two-sided z-test, flagging decline or improvement only when the change exceeds what the combined measurement uncertainty allows. Deliberately conservative: anything smaller stays unflagged, because it could be noise. The voice finding is stress-tested twice more — it survives the stricter 95% bar and an alternative time window — and the full method, code, and data ship in the repository, reproducible offline with one command.

02What this is

An open, reproducible evidence commons on two pillars — not a dashboard of opinions. Every claim carries its grade and the exact condition under which it would be withdrawn.

The Evidence Ledger

41 graded, falsifiable evidence entries (ROBUST CONTESTED REFUTED), each with a pre-registered falsification trigger and a machine-checked structure. A generated falsification watch gathers every trigger in one place, and each entry’s next-review date goes stale loudly, not silently.

The Forecast

A calibration layer, not a hero call: pre-registered backsliding forecasts, scored against a no-change baseline — honest enough to tell you it doesn’t beat that baseline yet (see § 03).

The Indicators

Governance change across six dimensions, computed with honest uncertainty — then triangulated against V-Dem and Freedom House to publish divergence, never a single score.

The Simulators

Seven validated mechanisms of social change — each reproduces a published result via a unit test (threshold and network cascades, the committed-minority tipping point, reverse dominance, complex contagion, the legitimacy tipping point, and bounded-confidence opinion dynamics).

Self-auditing

Every public number is machine-checked — structure, freshness, an overstatement guard, a count-consistency gate, and a spatial-floor gate that blocks any data below country level — all reproducible offline, one command, no network. Beyond the gates, the whole corpus has been put through adversarial review: every citation web-verified, every formula independently re-derived.

What is tested

A tiering map of every claim, from machine-verified down to honestly untestable. Nothing claims a tier it hasn’t earned.

What the grades mean — and what would change our minds
ROBUST
Verified against primary sources; uncontested, or convergent across independent lines of evidence.
CONTESTED
Real evidence on both sides — replication failures or unresolved expert dispute. Reported as live, not settled.
REFUTED
Fails verification. Kept on the record so no one builds on it twice.
OPEN
The evidence does not yet answer the question — and we say so.

Every entry also pre-registers its falsification trigger — the specific observation that would downgrade it. Meet a trigger with evidence and the grade moves: in public, dated, and credited. That is the difference between an evidence commons and advocacy.

How to check us — without trusting us

Trust should be checkable, not requested. The signals, each verifiable on your own machine, offline: self-correction on the record — a REFUTED grade kept in public view, a forecast whose published headline is its own negative result, and adversarial review rounds whose findings against our own work are documented with their fixes. Byte-exact reproduction — one command recomputes everything from pinned snapshots with no network and fails on any drift between code, data, and prose. Pre-registered falsifiability — every entry’s “what would change our minds” collected in one watchlist. Uncertainty as the headline — the noise floor is the most prominent number we publish, and disagreement between instruments is surfaced, never blended. No funder to please — currently unfunded and independent; if that ever changes, disclosure comes first.

The full list, one verification command per signal, ships in the repo as docs/TRUST_SIGNALS.md. The one-command version: make verify-offline.

03What honesty looks like in a forecast

Anyone can publish a scary prediction. The hard, trustworthy thing is to score yourself in the open. So the forecasting pillar runs 2,600 pre-registered backsliding forecasts and grades them with proper scoring rules against two dead-simple baselines — assume no change, or just predict the long-run base rate — then reports the result plainly:

Our models beat neither baseline yet — and we say so, in public.

That isn’t a weakness to bury; it is the product. The bar to clear is written down before the scoring, and a future model earns its place only by beating it on the same open harness. A project that will tell you when its own forecast isn’t good enough is one you can believe when it says something is.

04How it works — the lighthouse, not the dragnet

Durable change comes when a committed minority reaches a critical mass and a cascade spreads — but that cascade runs on trusted, reinforcing signal, exactly what disinformation destroys. So we don’t aggregate or surveil people. We build a true, open, reusable thing that aligned people find and gather around, voluntarily. Supplying a missing, trusted information feedback loop — what open, falsifiable science is — is a foundation a small team can actually build; it crosses into action only once it is also made salient and taken up in real work. It is a necessary seed, not a sufficient cause: organizing and solidarity are coequal, never downstream.

05The method, demonstrated

We hold the same bar to ourselves and to others — adversarial, steelmanned, graded. As a worked example, we ran the verification on a famous set of public claims: of Graham Hancock’s ten major claims, 0 were supported and 5 refuted — while fairly crediting the few real things mainstream science has had to update on. The discipline, not the verdict, is the product.

06The one rule

Watch power, not people.

Everything here measures the conduct of institutions and power — never individuals. No surveillance, no predictive policing, no targeting. An instrument built to defend open societies must never become one that could serve their enemies. And because a covenant alone is non-binding on open licences, it is backed by an enforced threat model: a written gate decides what may be computed and published — protection lives in the design, not just the promise.

07Open by design

Multi-licensed and reproducible: prose under CC BY-SA 4.0, code under GPL-3.0, derived data under CC0. The whole project runs as one stdlib-only command — ./pharos browses the graded entries, prints the indicators and the forecast, and re-runs the audits — offline, no install, no network. Everything recomputes from pinned data and self-audits before every change. Fork it, check it, build on it, prove it wrong. The strongest contribution is meeting a claim’s falsification trigger with evidence.
