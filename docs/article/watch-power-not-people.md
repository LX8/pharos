# Watch Power, Not People

### Open, honest evidence against the authoritarian tide — and how a committed minority can use it

*Why the measurable backsliding of the last decade is specifically about **voice**,
why most of what you read about it is over-claimed, and how trustworthy,
reproducible measurement can become the reinforcing signal a movement actually
needs.*

> **Status:** working draft (2026-06-02). Everything here is reproducible from
> open data and open code; every number can be regenerated with one command, and
> every claim is graded and carries a condition under which it would be withdrawn.
> Licensed CC BY-SA 4.0. Bound by one rule — *watch power, not people.*

---

## The short version

1. **The decade's governance data, read honestly, says something specific.**
   Across six World Bank governance dimensions and ~204 countries, 2014→2024,
   **88% of changes are within measurement noise** — most countries did not move
   beyond their own error bars. Of the changes that *are* statistically real,
   declines outnumber improvements 88 to 54. But the erosion is **not**
   across-the-board: it is concentrated in **Voice & Accountability** — free
   expression, free media, participation, the accountability of power — while
   corruption control actually improved in more countries than it worsened. The
   authoritarian turn shows up first, and most clearly, as the **silencing of
   voice**.

2. **Change at scale is real and it cascades — but not the way the myths say.**
   A committed minority can tip a whole system; this is among the better-supported
   findings in social science. But there is no magic number, cascades are rare and
   beatable, and the "energy" that spreads them is not mystical — it is *trusted,
   repeated, reinforcing signal* moving through tight social networks.

3. **That is exactly what disinformation destroys — and exactly what honest, open
   measurement can supply.** The reason movements are succeeding *less* often than
   they did a generation ago is, in part, that the shared information ecosystem has
   been poisoned. So the highest-leverage thing a journalist, organizer, or builder
   can do is become a source of *true, checkable, reinforcing* signal. This piece
   is an argument for that — and a small working example of it.

If you take one thing from this: **the credibility is the strategy.** In an
information war, the side that can be checked, that names its own uncertainty, and
that tells you when it might be wrong, is the side that earns the durable trust a
cascade runs on.

## Part I — The finding, and why honesty leads

Start with the data, because for this audience the data is the hook — and how we
*handle* it is the whole argument.

The World Bank's **Worldwide Governance Indicators (WGI)** score every country, on
a 0–100 scale, across six dimensions: Voice & Accountability, Rule of Law, Control
of Corruption, Government Effectiveness, Regulatory Quality, and Political
Stability. Crucially, the Bank publishes a **90% confidence interval** for each
score — an honest admission that these are *estimates built from many perception
sources*, not exact counts. Most coverage throws that uncertainty away. We keep it.

Here is the rule we used: compare each country's score in 2014 to 2024, and call
the change a real **decline** or **improvement** *only* when it is larger than the
combined measurement uncertainty would allow (a two-sided test at the 90% level).
Anything smaller, we leave unflagged — because it could be noise.

The result, across 1,222 country-by-dimension comparisons:

| | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Within measurement noise** | **1,080** | **88%** |
| Meaningful decline | 88 | 7% |
| Meaningful improvement | 54 | 4% |

The first row is the most important and the least reported: **most governance
scores did not move beyond their error bars in a decade.** If a piece tells you
"democracy is collapsing everywhere," it is reading noise as signal. The honest
story is narrower and more useful.

Because when you look at *where* the real movement is, a pattern jumps out:

| Dimension | mean 2014→2024 | declined | improved |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **Voice & Accountability** | 56.9 → 55.0 | **33** | 6 |
| **Political Stability / Absence of Violence** | 66.3 → 65.3 | 19 | 7 |
| Rule of Law | 55.9 → 56.0 | 13 | 13 |
| Regulatory Quality | 54.1 → 54.3 | 7 | 7 |
| Government Effectiveness | 52.6 → 52.1 | 7 | 4 |
| Control of Corruption | 46.3 → 47.1 | 9 | **17** |

The measurable backsliding of the decade is **specifically about voice** — by a
5.5-to-1 margin — not about whether states can pave roads or collect taxes (mostly
flat), and not about corruption (which improved in more places than it worsened).
The worst declines in Voice & Accountability are exactly where you would expect:
Nicaragua, Mali, Tunisia, Afghanistan, Hong Kong, Myanmar, Russia. The clearest
improvements are real democratic openings: **The Gambia** after Jammeh, Armenia,
Sri Lanka.

### Read it dimension by dimension

A single blended "governance score" would hide the most important pattern of all.
Take Saudi Arabia. Over the decade it **meaningfully improved on four dimensions** —
Control of Corruption (+20 points), Government Effectiveness (+14), Rule of Law
(+11), Regulatory Quality (+8) — while **Voice & Accountability stayed flat at
around 28 out of 100**, among the lowest scores on Earth. A composite index would
show Saudi Arabia *rising* and call it progress. Read dimension by dimension, the
truth is sharper and more familiar: a state can become *more effective, more
orderly, less corrupt* while political voice stays crushed. That is the signature of
competent autocracy, and it is invisible to any measure that averages voice together
with administration. (Even those technical "improvements" deserve a skeptic's eye —
in a closed system, a *perception* of less corruption can track a politically
selective crackdown as easily as genuine reform.) The lesson for anyone using
governance data: never trust one number where six honest ones, each with its
uncertainty, will tell you *what kind* of change you are looking at.

### The caveat that proves the method

Now the part most write-ups would bury, and that we put up front: **one of these
dimensions will mislead you, and we will tell you which.**

"Political Stability / Absence of Violence" sounds like it measures something good.
It does not always. It rewards *the absence of unrest* — which a sufficiently
repressive regime supplies. Its computed "improvements" over the decade include
**North Korea** and, bizarrely, **Ukraine** (an artifact of relative scoring and a
disrupted baseline). A regime that crushes dissent into silence can *rise* on this
measure. So we flag it explicitly: **stability is not freedom; do not read
Political Stability gains as democratic gains.** We report the dimension only for
completeness.

That is the entire ethic in one example. A measure that can mislead is named as
such, in public, before anyone uses it. *Watch power, not people* — and watch your
own instruments too.

## Part II — Why this matters to you: the cascade

Here is the question an organizer or a reporter should ask next: *so what?* A
dashboard does not free a single prisoner. Why should anyone fighting for an open
society care about confidence intervals?

Because of how open societies are actually won and lost — which, it turns out, is
something science can speak to with unusual confidence.

### Committed minorities really can tip whole systems

This is not wishful thinking; it is one of the more robust results across several
independent research traditions:

- **Granovetter's threshold models** (1978) showed that collective action turns on
  the *distribution* of people's tipping points, so that nearly identical crowds
  can produce a riot or nothing at all.
- **Watts's cascade model** (2002) showed that system-wide change spreads when a
  shock reaches a connected cluster of "easily moved" people — and that such
  cascades, while *rare*, can be enormous.
- **Centola and colleagues** (2018) ran controlled experiments in which a committed
  minority of about **25%** could flip an established social convention — a result
  robust enough to survive *quadrupling* the incentives to resist it.
- **Chenoweth and Stephan's** data on a century of resistance campaigns found that
  movements mobilizing roughly **3.5%** of a population in sustained, nonviolent
  participation essentially never failed.

And it scales to the largest problems: **Otto and colleagues** (2020) used exactly
this framework to map "social tipping interventions" for stabilizing the global
climate. The mechanism your instinct reaches for — *get enough of the right people
aligned and the system flips* — is real, and it operates at civilizational scale.

### But three corrections, or you will get it wrong

The popular version of this idea is dangerous precisely because the real version is
true. Three corrections separate them:

1. **There is no magic number.** The "25%" and "3.5%" findings come from specific
   contexts; measured thresholds range from under 1% to well over half a
   population. *Who* and *where* in a network the committed people are matters as
   much as how many. Chasing a percentage is a category error.

2. **It is not automatic.** Cascades are *rare*; most efforts fizzle. And they are
   beatable. Nonviolent-campaign success has fallen from roughly **65% in the 1990s
   to under 34% in the 2010s**, as authoritarian regimes learned to counter them
   with disinformation, surveillance, and cross-border repression. Reaching a
   threshold guarantees nothing.

3. **The "energy" is not mystical — and the mystical versions will discredit you.**
   The single most useful result here is **Centola and Macy's "complex contagion"**
   (2007): unlike a virus, which you can catch from one contact, a *behavior* or a
   *belief* usually requires **multiple, reinforcing exposures from people you
   trust** before you adopt it. That is why social change travels through tight,
   high-trust clusters with bridges between them — not through scattered numbers or
   viral one-shots. "Shared energy," operationalized, means *reinforcement +
   trust + network structure.* All measurable. All buildable.

   And to be unambiguous, because activists and writers reach for these and get
   burned: the **"hundredth monkey"** (instant, mystical spread once a threshold is
   crossed) is a *fabrication* — its own author admitted he "embellished the facts."
   So is Sheldrake's "morphic resonance," and so are claims that group meditation
   lowers crime through a consciousness field. Use any of these and you hand your
   opponents your own discrediting. The real mechanism is stronger *because* it is
   true.

### The connection — and the whole reason for this project

Put the two halves together. **Change cascades on trusted, reinforcing signal. The
defining feature of the present moment is that the shared signal has been poisoned.**

That is why movements that would have tipped a generation ago now stall. Complex
contagion needs sources people trust and can check; disinformation works by
flooding the zone until *nothing* can be trusted or checked. The authoritarian
playbook is not only censorship — it is the manufacture of doubt, so that the
reinforcing signal a movement needs can never cohere.

Which tells you where the leverage is. The systems scientist **Donella Meadows**
ranked the places one can intervene in a complex system. Near the very top of what
an individual can actually reach is *"the structure of information flows"* — and her
favorite example is suggestive: after the U.S. *required factories to publish their
toxic emissions* — no fines, no bans, just the numbers, made visible — reported
emissions fell about **40%**. (Honesty check: how much of that drop was the
disclosure itself versus the co-pollutant regulation running alongside it is
genuinely disputed, and Meadows offered her leverage ranking as an *untested
heuristic*, not a measured law. Take it as an illustration, not proof.)

**Trustworthy, open, checkable information is a load-bearing input for the part of
the fight that is fought over shared facts** — and supplying it is a genuinely
available, high-leverage move for a journalist, a researcher, a small team: *one of
several*, with organizing and coalition-building coequal, not downstream. It is a
*necessary seed, not a sufficient cause* — lone signals cascade rarely (Watts 2002).
The proof of concept makes the point honestly: **Our World in Data** did come to
anchor reporting worldwide — but over roughly 5–7 years, with a funded team and
institutional backing. The lesson is "start solo," not "solo is enough."

## Part III — The method: honesty as a weapon

So how do you build signal that can survive an information war? You make it
*checkable, falsifiable, and honest about its own limits* — to a degree that
propaganda structurally cannot match. Three practices do most of the work.

**1. Cite only what is measured.** Every claim ships with its source and its
uncertainty. The governance finding above does not say "voice is collapsing"; it
says *exactly* how many countries moved beyond their error bars, and admits that
88% did not. This is not hedging. It is the move that makes you unkillable in an
argument: you have already conceded everything an honest critic could take, so what
remains is solid ground.

**2. Grade every claim, and pre-commit to how you could be proven wrong.** The
companion to this article is an open **evidence ledger** — a set of short, sourced
entries on what is actually known about containing authoritarianism, each marked
**ROBUST**, **CONTESTED**, **REFUTED**, or **OPEN**, and each carrying a
*falsification trigger*: the specific finding that would force us to downgrade it.
A few examples, exactly as they stand:

- *Psychological "prebunking" raises people's resistance to manipulation at scale.*
  **ROBUST** — but paired with its honest twin: *it does not reliably transfer to
  telling true from false, and sometimes fails to replicate.* **CONTESTED.** We do
  not let you walk away with only the encouraging half.
- *Whether authoritarian-populism is driven by status threat or economic anxiety.*
  **OPEN** — a live, unresolved dispute, labeled as such, not laundered into a
  talking point.
- *Intergroup contact reduces prejudice.* **ROBUST** as a correlation — *but the
  effect shrinks in the most rigorous studies and has never once been measured for
  interracial contact among adults over 25.* **CONTESTED.**

This is the antidote to *two* failure modes at once: the disinformation that says
nothing is true, and the well-meaning advocacy that ships unreplicated hope as
settled fact. A ledger that downgrades its own entries when the evidence turns is
the opposite of propaganda, and readers can feel the difference.

**3. Watch power, not people.** Every measure here is pitched at *states,
institutions, and the documented conduct of power* — never at individuals. This is
a hard line, not a slogan. The same techniques that flag a government's slide could,
inverted, build a watch-list; the same forecast that warns a newsroom could, in the
wrong hands, target a dissident. So the design refuses person-level resolution by
construction, and the project's licence carries an ethical covenant against
surveillance, predictive policing, and targeting. **An instrument built to defend
open societies must never become one that could serve their enemies.** Two honest
caveats keep this from being a slogan: aggregation defeats *individual*
re-identification, but *group- and place-level* targeting is the harder, still-open
axis (it is precisely the predictive-policing failure mode), and a covenant in a
permissive open licence is a request, not a binding restriction. So the real
protection lives in *what we choose to compute and publish* — governed by an
explicit, enforced threat model — not in the licence text alone.

### And the limits, stated plainly

Honesty about method demands honesty about what it *cannot* do:

- **A dashboard is not a movement.** This is the oldest trap in the book —
  "techno-solutionism," the belief that the right tool fixes a political problem.
  It does not. Measurement *informs and equips* the human work of organizing,
  reporting, and solidarity; it never replaces it.
- **The "warning-response gap" is real.** Knowing a country is sliding does not stop
  the slide — only people with the will and the standing to act do that. So the
  point of open signal is to reach *those people*, not to assume that visibility
  alone moves power.
- **Goodhart's Law lurks.** The moment a metric becomes a target, people game it. So
  no single number is offered as a goal; the numbers are a lens, with their
  uncertainty attached, not a scoreboard.
- **Honest measurement reaches the reachable.** Open evidence persuades those still
  open to evidence. It is necessary, not sufficient. It does not, by itself, reach
  the deeply captured.

None of this weakens the case. It *is* the case. The credibility you earn by saying
all of this out loud is the precise asset disinformation cannot counterfeit.

### How this differs from the indices you already know

You may be thinking: don't V-Dem, Freedom House, and the EIU already do this? They
do — and this is built to *stand on them*, not replace them. Three honest
distinctions:

- **It carries the uncertainty all the way through.** Most index coverage collapses
  an estimate to a single number and a rank. The entire point here is to refuse
  that — to flag only what exceeds the published error bars, and to say out loud
  when a move is noise. That discipline is the contribution; the raw scores are the
  World Bank's.
- **It grades and can retract.** The evidence ledger is not a leaderboard; it is a
  living record of *what is known and how confidently*, built to be downgraded when
  the evidence turns. No index publishes its own falsification conditions.
- **It is fully reproducible, forkable, and watches power not people.** Every number
  regenerates from open code and open data with one command; anyone can audit or
  extend it; and it is bound by a covenant against any person-level use.

The strongest move, in fact, is to **triangulate.** If V-Dem's expert coding,
Freedom House's assessments, and the World Bank's perception-based scores *all*
locate the decade's erosion in voice and accountability, that agreement across
independent instruments is far harder to dismiss than any one of them alone.
Cross-checking this finding against those sources is the explicit next step — and an
open invitation to anyone reading.

### Verify it yourself

This matters more than any single claim, so be concrete: the entire result is one
command away. Clone the repository and run `make verify`. It runs the offline unit
tests on the core calculation, then pulls the current World Bank governance data and
**recomputes every figure in this article from scratch.** The per-country table, the
code, the confidence intervals, and the method are all open. You are not asked to
trust the conclusion — you are invited to regenerate it, move the baseline year, add
a dimension, or try to break it. That standing invitation to check and to falsify is
what a trustworthy signal looks like, and it is the one thing the manufacture of
doubt cannot imitate.

## Part IV — What you can actually do with this

This is written for people who *act* — so here is the practical part.

**If you are a journalist or editor:** you have, in the governance indicator, a
source you can cite without fear of over-claiming, because the over-claiming has
already been stripped out. Lead with the honest frame — *most countries did not
measurably move; the real decline is concentrated in voice* — and you are both more
accurate and more defensible than the doom-or-denial framings your readers are
drowning in. The full per-country numbers, the code, and the methodology are open;
you can check us, and you can show your readers that you did.

**If you are an organizer or activist:** the value here is *reinforcing signal your
people can trust.* Complex contagion needs sources that survive scrutiny; a fact
that collapses under the first hostile question doesn't reinforce belief, it drains
it. Build your case on claims that are graded and checkable, route them to the
specific actors with the motive and standing to move, and concentrate on the dense,
high-trust clusters where adoption actually spreads. Five connected, convinced,
networked people matter more than five thousand passive followers — that is not a
metaphor, it is the mechanism.

**If you are a builder, researcher, or technologist:** the strongest move is to make
this *yours.* The evidence ledger and the indicator pipeline are open and
reproducible — fork them, extend the dimensions, **cross-check the "voice" finding
against independent sources like V-Dem and Freedom House** (does a second
measurement system agree the decline is specifically in voice?), and connect your
work to the existing open infrastructure — Uppsala's **ViEWS** conflict
early-warning system, ACLED, V-Dem — rather than rebuilding it. Every honest,
forkable artifact is another bridge between clusters, another reinforcing signal in
the network.

**And for anyone:** the cascade does not start with a number. It starts with the
first person willing to put something *true and reusable* into the world and stand
on it, in the open, persistently. You cannot be the mythical "hundredth monkey." But
you can be the **first** — real, documented, checkable — which is exactly the
mechanism the science confirms. The lighthouse is not built by the crowd that
gathers around it. It is built first, and lit, and then the crowd can find it.

## Appendix A — Methodology

For each country *c* and governance dimension *d*, let `s₁`, `s₂` be the 0–100 WGI
score in 2014 and 2024, each accompanied by the World Bank's published 90%
confidence interval `[lb, ub]`. The standard error implied by that interval is
`se = (ub − lb) / (2 × 1.6449)`. The decade change `Δ = s₂ − s₁` is tested against
its combined uncertainty with a two-sided z-statistic
`z = Δ / √(se₁² + se₂²)`, and flagged:

- **DECLINE** if `z ≤ −1.6449`, **IMPROVE** if `z ≥ +1.6449` (the 90% bar),
- **NO_SIG_CHANGE** otherwise.

The bar is deliberately conservative: a country with no flag has *not* been shown
to be stable — its movement simply cannot be distinguished from measurement error
at 90% confidence. The calculation is a pure function with offline unit tests;
`make verify` runs the tests and regenerates every number from the live World Bank
API, so the result is reproducible end to end. Source: World Bank Worldwide
Governance Indicators (source 3), latest year 2024; indicator codes
`GOV_WGI_{VA,RL,CC,GE,RQ,PV}.SC` with `.SC_LB` / `.SC_UB`.

**Known limits.** WGI is a *perception-based composite* of expert and survey
sources; it measures perceptions, with real uncertainty (which is why the interval
is carried through). Scores are partly *relative* across countries. The "Political
Stability" dimension conflates stability with absence of violence and should not be
read as a democracy measure (see Part I). Unweighted country means are a rough
gauge, not population-weighted. Nothing here is a causal claim — it is measurement,
to be paired with the evidence ledger for explanation.
